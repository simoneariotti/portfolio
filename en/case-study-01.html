Askme Design System: from zero to AI-ready

Three years of a system built from scratch for an enterprise suite, and the strategy to make it readable by artificial intelligence.

01 · Context

Three products, one designer, no system.

Askme is a B2B enterprise suite for business process automation: service management, legally binding e-signature and native conversational AI, adopted by organisations in finance, manufacturing, healthcare and public administration. When I joined as product designer, the three modules had grown independently: no shared components, no common language, no design system.

I built it from scratch and have owned it for three years: variables, shared components, cross-module patterns and the branding guidelines that sit on top. This case study covers two arcs: building the system, and the work in progress to make it readable by AI.

02 · The problem

Divergence, then divergence at the speed of AI.

Three products, three histories, one brand. Every new feature reinvented patterns that already existed somewhere else in the suite. Consistency held at the level of a single feature, cracked at module level, and broke completely between one module and another.

Then a second problem arrived, a faster one: AI-assisted development let teams ship new interfaces without going through design. The output was usable, but every generated screen used its own patterns, duplicating components that already existed. Divergence at the speed of AI.

03 · The reasoning

Patterns that survive futures nobody has drawn yet.

Building the system: the first three years

The Askme component library started from nothing. Every piece answered an immediate need, but was already shaped by the system that would grow around it. It is dense work up front that pays back later: adding a feature rarely meant rewriting the ones before it.

From file upload to cross-module navigation, from form controls to Sign signatures: a whole library, still in production today. Two examples tell it better than a list.

  • Adaptable containers. Containers, dialogs and expansion panels built as archetypes: they hold data, images, complex cards, inputs, anything. Not one component per case, but a single form that holds cases nobody has specified yet. Same containers, always different contents.
  • A table that removes noise. Tables are everywhere in Askme. The custom one, with advanced filters and saved views, gives every user or their manager the view they need: more focus on their own work, less noise, always the same recognisable pattern. On mobile it degrades into usable cards, deliberately leaving the more complex functions out.
  • A bridge between design and engineering. A design system only holds if both sides speak the same language. One shared library, naming that matches, decisions made together with the developers rather than handed down: much of a system's value lives in exactly that hinge.

Making it readable by AI: the work in progress

The same AI push that fuels the divergence is what makes a system AI can read necessary.

If everyone uses their own AI with their own patterns, divergence multiplies. If the AI reads the design system, that same speed becomes convergence. The strategy I proposed and am now executing:

  • Portability before lock-in. Foundations converted to the W3C DTCG standard for tokens: readable by any tool, not trapped in a single vendor.
  • Two layers, two owners. A semantic layer (design's, tool-independent) kept strictly separate from a code-binding layer (engineering's). They never mix.
  • An automated token pipeline. From the design tool to a versioned repository through to generated code, with automatic pull requests. One source of truth instead of two that slowly drift apart.
  • Components explained to a machine. Naming that matches the code library, semantic descriptions, reference implementations: so the AI picks the right existing component instead of inventing a lookalike.
  • An explicit boundary between core and extension. What every module shares and what belongs to one alone, declared rather than implied.

The strategy closes with a falsifiable pilot: given only the design system, can an AI compose a new, consistent module? Explicit success criteria, with the stated freedom to return an honest “not yet”. Presenting it as an experiment rather than a promise is what makes the proposal credible.

04 · Selected work

The patterns, not the interfaces.

05 · Outcome and principles

One language, and the strategy in execution.

Three products now share a single design language, one component library across design and code, and branding guidelines built into the system rather than parked beside it. The AI-ready strategy was written as a single document for two audiences at once, a proposal for the people who decide and a reference guide for the people who execute, and is now being executed.

A principle that emerged

The value of a design system is not order: it is the speed between an idea and its coherent structure. Everything else is a means.
